Using equity to grow a property portfolio

How usable equity works, and how it's commonly used to fund a next purchase.

What is usable equity?

Your equity is the gap between your property's current market value and what you still owe on it. Lenders won't let you borrow against all of that gap — they cap what's called your usable equity to protect against market movements.

A common formula: Many lenders will lend up to 80% of a property's current valuation, minus the remaining loan balance.

Example: A home valued at $800,000 — 80% of that is $640,000. With a $400,000 loan balance remaining, usable equity comes to roughly $240,000, which could potentially be drawn out as a deposit for a further purchase.

How the equity cycle typically works

01

Property value grows

You hold a property in a solid growth area — Brisbane's Southside, for example — and its value rises over time while your loan balance stays roughly stable.

02

An equity top-up is arranged

A top-up loan releases the usable equity into a separate account, which can then be used toward the deposit and costs on a further purchase.

03

A second asset is acquired

You now hold two properties, each building equity independently — though this also means twice the exposure to market movement and interest rate changes.

Managing the risk

Borrowing against equity is still borrowing, so it's worth building in safeguards:

  • Keep a buffer. Holding some of the released equity in an offset account gives you a cushion for vacancies or unexpected repairs.
  • Stress-test serviceability. Make sure your household income and rental returns could still cover repayments if interest rates rise.
