Mortgage terms, in plain English

The jargon lenders use, translated — bookmark this one.

LVR (Loan-to-Value Ratio)
Your loan amount as a percentage of the property's value. A $450,000 loan on a $600,000 property is a 75% LVR. Lower LVR generally means better rates and no LMI.
LMI (Lenders Mortgage Insurance)
A one-off insurance premium, usually required when your LVR is above 80%. It protects the lender, not you, if you default — but it's what allows many buyers to purchase with a smaller deposit.
Pre-approval
A lender's conditional indication of how much they'd likely lend you, based on the information you've supplied so far. It's not a guarantee — final approval still depends on the specific property and a full assessment.
Settlement
The day ownership of the property formally transfers to you and the loan funds are released to the seller. This is the finish line of the purchase process.
Offset account
A transaction account linked to your loan; its balance reduces the amount you're charged interest on, while remaining fully accessible to you.
Redraw facility
Allows you to withdraw extra repayments you've made above the minimum required amount.
Discharge fee
A fee some lenders charge when you fully pay off or refinance away from a loan, covering their administrative cost of closing it.
Serviceability
A lender's assessment of whether your income can comfortably cover the loan repayments, alongside your other expenses and debts — usually tested at a higher "buffer" rate than the actual rate you'll pay.
Comparison rate
A rate that combines a loan's interest rate with most of its standard fees, intended to make it easier to compare the true cost between different loan products.
Genuine savings
Funds you've accumulated yourself over time (typically 3+ months), as distinct from a gift or a one-off deposit — some lenders require proof of this, particularly for higher-LVR loans.
Equity
The difference between your property's current market value and what you still owe on it.
Cross-collateralisation
Using more than one property as security for a single loan, or for loans across multiple properties. It can limit your flexibility later, which is why many brokers try to structure around it.
Conveyancing
The legal process of transferring property ownership, usually handled by a solicitor or licensed conveyancer working alongside your broker and lender.
Stamp duty (transfer duty)
A state government tax charged on most property purchases, calculated on the purchase price or market value — first home buyers may be eligible for a concession or exemption.
